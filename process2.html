Process Analysis 2: IAT-265 Final Project

This project was the final assignment for my IAT-265 course, Multimedia Programming for Art and Design. This was an individual project to be completed over multiple weeks and progress checks where we were supposed to create a short program that guides the user through a creative scenario of our choice, noting that there should be no losing condition since this isn't necessarily a game. This is designed to test our knowledge of the major concepts of Java programming we learned and practiced throughout the term, and making several of them work together to achieve the desired result while putting our own creative spin on it.

Link to Project Video Report

For my project, I chose to make the scenario about repairing a battle-damaged robot with multiple steps involved, such as re-attaching an arm, and recharging its battery. I originally wanted to create 3 distinct tasks, but scaled it back to 2 for time constraints. One struggle I had during the process of this assignment was figuring out how to apply concepts introduced later in the course, such as factories and perlin noise, so my solutions mostly came down to asking my TAs for assistance as well as reading over the readings/examples provided. Time constraints were a big issue in general, but I ultimately managed to get the project finished, albeit with some elements needing to be scaled back, mainly the art assets.

For as many challenges as this project had, it still taught me a lot about how to navigate relatively large programming assignments similar to it, as well as helping me better understand concepts I might not be proficient in. While the art needed to be simplified to meet the project's deadline, my knowledge in visual design principles and organizing elements from previous courses such as IAT-102 and IAT-100 helped me design the look and sound of this program to be easy to navigate and understand, something that can carry over into future projects and fields with those same requirements.
